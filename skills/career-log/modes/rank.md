# Mode: rank

Sort a list of employers (a career fair roster, a job board export, a set of postings) into tiers the user can act on, and give each worthwhile one an opening line backed by the log.

1. **Load the rules.** Read `direction.md` in full: the role ranking, the sorting order under Employer preferences, the Constraints, and What to emphasise, by target. When work authorisation is unanswered, ask the user whether any employer is ruled out by it (a visa they need sponsored, a citizenship or clearance they lack), at the point of need ([SKILL.md › Settings](../SKILL.md#settings)): it decides what step 2 researches. When the target roles are unanswered, ask for them the same way: step 2 looks for teams in those areas and step 3 measures domain fit against them. When they are skipped or the user declines, use the areas the entries' tags show most instead, and say so in the report. Search the folder names in `outputs/applications/` and `applications.md` for each listed employer, for earlier contact; never read `applications.md` whole.
2. **Research each employer.** Before researching, grep `outputs/reports/*-ranking.md` for each name and read the newest earlier ranking that has it: re-check its hard-constraint facts (sponsorship, citizenship or export-control clearance, location) on the web, since they gate the tier, and carry the rest over with the URLs it cites and its date (`<fact> (<URL>, as of <earlier ranking's date>)`), not citing the report itself. Research a carried item afresh only when the user asks, or when the earlier ranking has a `GAP:` for it. For every employer with no earlier ranking, and for every item not carried over, look up from the web, citing each fact's URL, every item below:
   - what it builds, and which teams hire engineers in the user's target areas;
   - each hard constraint `direction.md › Constraints` answers: whether the employer meets it (for a visa the user needs, whether it sponsors that visa; US H-1B filing history, for example, is public data; for a location, where the roles are). A skipped or unanswered constraint is not researched. Whatever Constraints says, note whether its roles need citizenship or export-control clearance;
   - size and stage.
   Each item, and each part of one (a team, a division, an office), ends in one of two forms: a fact with its URL, or a `GAP:` naming what was searched for and not found. Every employer gets every item in one of those forms, the size, stage and citizenship or export-control note included; an item not yet searched for has neither, so search for it before step 4.

   When the list is long, research in parallel with subagents ([SKILL.md › Subagents](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)), each with a share of the employers; otherwise research one employer after another. Each prompt's first line is `Read <root>/skills/career-log/modes/rank.md whole and do only its step 2, earlier rankings included, for these employers: <names>. Reply with every item for each employer, each a fact with its URL or a GAP:. Steps 1 and 3 to 6 are the caller's: ask the user nothing and write no file.`, with `<root>` as an absolute path. After it come step 1's results, as data: the target areas (or the entry tags used in their place), the answered constraints with their values, and the work authorisation answer. Check every reply for both forms before step 3, and research yourself what a reply left out.
3. **Score in the order `direction.md` gives.** Hard constraints first, as a gate: an employer that breaks one in `direction.md › Constraints` (it will not sponsor the visa the user needs, its roles need a citizenship or clearance the user lacks) is out, whatever else it offers. An employer whose roles need a citizenship or clearance that the work authorisation answer does not mention is not out: list the requirement in its row as something to check. Then the sorting order under `direction.md › Employer preferences`. When it is unanswered, ask the user for it before scoring, at the point of need ([SKILL.md › Settings](../SKILL.md#settings)), proposing domain fit against `direction.md › Target roles, ranked` (or the entries' areas, as step 1 says), then size and pay. When it is skipped or the user declines, score in that proposed order and say so in the report.
4. **Write the tiers.** For each employer: tier, one-line reason, the evidence for each hard constraint, and for every employer that is not out an opening line that names the entry to lead with (from `direction.md › What to emphasise`; an entry outside the recency window ([../writing.md](../writing.md) › Recency, or the window `direction.md › What to emphasise` sets for every target) only when it is the only evidence for what the employer hires for) and one question to ask them. An opening line is one sentence the user can say aloud at the booth, built from the entry's facts, read in the entry itself this run (an earlier ranking's opening lines and claims about the user are never copied: step 2 carries over only employer facts) (in the shape "I <what the entry shows I did, with its result>, and I'd like to do that on your <team or product>"), not an entry name or a list of keywords. Every claim about the user traces to an entry.
5. **Show the ranking and stop.** Show the user the whole ranking, in full and unprompted, never a summary of it: for every employer its tier, reason, evidence for each hard constraint, and for every employer that is not out the opening line and the question, each written out. Before sending it, check it line by line and fix what fails: every employer on the list has its own row with a tier; every employer that is not out has its own complete opening line (never "same as above" or a variant described in words); every item and every part of one is a fact with its URL or a `GAP:` (a line that says, in any language, that something was "not checked", "not re-checked" or "not researched" is neither, and fails); every hard-constraint fact carried over from an earlier ranking was re-checked this run. Then wait for their reply. Nothing is written to a file, committed or pushed before it: until step 6 the ranking exists only in your message. Apply their changes and show the result again if they changed anything. Their reactions to companies are direction: record them in `direction.md`.
6. **Save,** only after step 5, to `outputs/reports/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<event>-ranking.md` with the sources, add the event to `applications.md › Events and contacts`, commit and push: `rank(<event>): <n> employers`.

Done when every employer on the list has a tier with evidence for each hard constraint or an explicit `GAP:`, every employer that is not out has an opening line traced to an entry, and the event is in `applications.md`.
