# Mode: rank

Before this mode's first step, run the **Start check** in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), once per session; a subagent sent to one step starts at that step.

Sort a list of employers (a career fair roster, a job board export, a set of postings) into tiers the user can act on, and give each worthwhile one an opening line backed by the log.

1. **Load the rules.** Read `direction.md` in full: the role ranking, the `**Sorting order` line under Employer preferences, the Constraints, and What to emphasise, by target. Work authorisation decides what step 2 researches; the target roles decide which teams step 2 looks for and what step 3 measures domain fit against. When work authorisation is empty ([SKILL.md › Setup](../SKILL.md#setup)), no employer is ruled out by it, and the report says so. When the target roles are empty, use the areas the entries' tags show most instead, and say so in the report. Search the folder names in `outputs/applications/` and `applications.md` for each listed employer, for earlier contact; never read `applications.md` whole.
2. **Research each employer.** Before researching, grep `outputs/reports/*-ranking.md` for each name and read the newest earlier ranking that has it: re-check its hard-constraint facts on the web (its authorisation language, its sponsorship record, where its roles are), since they gate the tier, and carry the rest over with the URLs it cites and its date (`<fact> (<URL>, as of <earlier ranking's date>)`), not citing the report itself. Research a carried item afresh only when the user asks, or when the earlier ranking has a `GAP:` for it. For every employer with no earlier ranking, and for every item not carried over, look up from the web, citing each fact's URL, every item below:
   - what it builds, and which teams hire engineers in the user's target areas;
   - each hard constraint `direction.md › Constraints` answers: whether the employer meets it (for a visa the user needs, whether it sponsors that visa; US H-1B filing history, for example, is public data; for a location, where the roles are). An empty constraint is not researched. Whatever Constraints says, note its **authorisation language**: the sponsorship, citizenship or export-control clearance wording on its postings and careers pages;
   - size and stage.

   Read each page with a plain fetch. Only when the fetch fails (an error such as 403, or a page without its content: built by script, a bot wall) is the page a Browser capture: follow [browser.md](../browser.md) for it, its sessions opened as *Running it* says (the command prints the CLI's skill first), in a session with no `--profile` (a careers page needs no sign-in) named exactly `rank-<employer>` (the employer's name in lowercase letters and digits, each other run of characters one hyphen, with none at either end, as in `rank-acme-co` for Acme & Co.) and closed when that employer is done, so researchers working in parallel never share one.

   Each item, and each part of one (a team, a division, an office), ends in one of two forms: a fact with its URL, or a `GAP:` naming what was searched for and not found. Every employer gets every item in one of those forms, the size, stage and citizenship or export-control note included; an item not yet searched for has neither, so search for it before step 4.

   When the list is long, research in parallel with subagents ([SKILL.md › Subagents](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)), each with a share of the employers; otherwise research one employer after another. Before starting them, run the check's `playwright-cli install --skills` line now if it printed one; its other playwright-cli lines wait until a reply needs a Browser capture ([browser.md › Check and install](../browser.md#check-and-install)). Each prompt's first line is `Read <root>/skills/career-log/modes/rank.md whole and do only its step 2, earlier rankings included, for these employers: <names>. Reply with every item for each employer, each a fact with its URL or a GAP:. Steps 1 and 3 to 6 are the caller's: ask the user nothing and write no file. Open every playwright-cli session with the command in browser.md › Running it, which prints the CLI's skill first; end your reply with the line `playwright-cli skill: read` that command prints, or with "playwright-cli: not used".`, with `<root>` as an absolute path. After it come step 1's results, as data: the target areas (or the entry tags used in their place), the constraints with their values, and work authorisation's value, or `empty`. Check every reply for both forms and for that last line before step 3; treat a reply without the line as unchecked, and research again yourself every fact of its employers that came from a page a plain fetch cannot read. Research yourself what a reply left out and every `GAP: no content from fetch` ([browser.md › Check and install](../browser.md#check-and-install)); then delete `.playwright-cli/`.
3. **Score in the order `direction.md` gives.** Hard constraints first, as a gate: an employer that breaks one in `direction.md › Constraints` (it will not sponsor the visa the user needs, its roles need a citizenship or clearance the user lacks) is out, whatever else it offers. An employer whose roles need a citizenship or clearance that the work authorisation answer does not mention is not out: list the requirement in its row as something to check. Then the sorting order: the `**Sorting order` line under `direction.md › Employer preferences`. With no such line, or only a `GAP:`, it is empty, whatever else that section holds: score by domain fit against `direction.md › Target roles, ranked` (or the entries' areas, as step 1 says), then size and pay, and say in the report that the sorting order is empty. The section's other lines never put an employer out (only Constraints does): a preference (a kind of team) breaks ties; a line that would rule employers out or set a minimum is listed, in the row of each employer it may affect, as something to check; a career-fair lesson (which booths to visit first, queues, entry times) goes in the report as a note for the fair, not into the tiers.
4. **Write the tiers.** For each employer: tier, one-line reason, the evidence for each hard constraint, and for every employer that is not out an opening line and one question to ask them. Every claim about the user traces to an entry. An opening line:
   - names the entry to lead with, from `direction.md › What to emphasise`; an entry outside the recency window ([../writing.md](../writing.md) › Recency, or the window `direction.md › What to emphasise` sets for every target) only when it is the only evidence for what the employer hires for;
   - is one sentence the user can say aloud at the booth, in the form "I <what the entry shows I did, with its result>, and I'd like to do that on your <team or product>", never an entry name or a list of keywords;
   - is written fresh from the entry's facts, read in the entry itself this run. Take nothing about the user from an earlier ranking, its opening lines included: step 2 carries over only employer facts.
5. **Show the ranking and stop.** Show the user the whole ranking, in full and unprompted, never a summary of it: for every employer its tier, reason, evidence for each hard constraint, and for every employer that is not out the opening line and the question, each written out. Before sending it, check it line by line and fix what fails: every employer on the list has its own row with a tier; every employer that is not out has its own complete opening line (never "same as above" or a variant described in words); every item and every part of one is a fact with its URL or a `GAP:` (a line that says, in any language, that something was "not checked", "not re-checked" or "not researched" is neither, and fails); every hard-constraint fact carried over from an earlier ranking was re-checked this run. Then wait for their reply. Nothing is written to a file, committed or pushed before it: until step 6 the ranking exists only in your message. Apply their changes and show the result again if they changed anything. Their reactions to companies are direction: record them in `direction.md`.
6. **Save,** only after step 5, to `outputs/reports/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<event>-ranking.md` with the sources, add the event to `applications.md › Events and contacts`, commit and push: `rank(<event>): <n> employers`.

Done when every employer on the list has a tier with evidence for each hard constraint or an explicit `GAP:`, every employer that is not out has an opening line traced to an entry, and the event is in `applications.md`.
