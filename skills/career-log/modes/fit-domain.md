# fit: domain run

The domain run of [fit.md](fit.md): what to learn for a domain, a kind of employer or a role family when no postings are saved for it ("what should I learn to work at <kind of company>?"). It collects a fresh sample through [scan](scan.md)'s exploratory query run and its Screen, then runs [fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md) steps 2 to 8 on that sample. `<scan>` is `python3 -I <root>/tools/scan.py` (`py -3 -I` on Windows); its `--help` gives each command's output, `explore`'s included.

1. **Collect** through scan's commands, with the domain as the query:
   1. `<scan> explore "<the domain, in the user's words>"`. `<dir>` is its `dir:` line as an absolute path, as [scan › The run's names](scan.md#the-runs-names) makes one. Its `read:` lines are the sources to read, each with its query URL; keep its `skip:` lines for this run's step 3. When it refuses (no source takes a query), say why and ask the user to paste postings; the run goes on with those alone (item 4).
   2. Scan steps 3 to 6 on `<dir>`, reading each `read:` line's source through its query URL, with the `window`, `screen_budget` and `fit_budget` of [scan › Settings](scan.md#settings). `scan-sources.md` keeps its lines as they are: scan step 3's `Read:` and `Fields:` lines are a scan run's to write. Step 6.2's reads are a posting's only text: its fetch, then a Browser capture; its posting-API stand-in is left out, so a posting neither read gives stays unreadable. A source scan step 3 stops is skipped too: keep its name and why for this run's step 3.
   3. Scan steps 7.2 and 7.3 for the postings of `<dir>/fit.jsonl`. Scan step 7.1 is left out: a map reused from a scan report has no rows. Each step 7.3 prompt adds the line `Also give each row a skill, as fit-several-postings.md step 3 says, and the file "tier": <the tier on its fit.jsonl line>.` Once they are back, delete `.playwright-cli/`.
   4. **Pasted postings** the user adds (now, or after this run's step 3) are captured and mapped as [fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md) step 3 says for a pasted one, with a tier as its step 1 gives it.
   5. **Sponsorship and wording** as [fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md) step 1 does them, for the postings the heatmap will hold: those of `<dir>/fit.jsonl`, each gate yes in `<dir>/screen/*.jsonl`, and the pasted ones; a posting's text is its capture.
2. **[fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md) steps 2 to 4** on the fit results in `<dir>/fit/` and the pasted postings' map files. Step 2's extraction and step 3's map of a fit result are its subagent's. Each subagent named its skills alone, so first make every `skill` in those files and in `<dir>/screen/*.jsonl` that asks for the same skill the same words, as step 3 says; then check the maps together in one `fit.py map --several` run, and add step 4's rows to them.
3. **Step 5,** with the heatmap given `<dir>/fit/*.jsonl`, the pasted map files and `<dir>/screen/*.jsonl`: a screened posting with no full map adds its rough rows. The chat shows the `Heatmap:` line (it states the sample), the `skew:` line when it printed one, and `Sources skipped: <each skip: line's source and each source step 1.2 skipped, with why>` (or `Sources skipped: none`), so the user can judge the plan and paste what was missed; then the legend with its rough postings, the table and the things to check, as step 5 says. Step 5's by-content tier notes and conditions include the `check` items of the fit results and of the Screen's gate yes results, each with its posting. The `flag:` lines go only in the report.
4. **Steps 6 to 8,** step 7's `<scope>` the domain as an ASCII name, made as step 7 says. The report holds the `Sources skipped:` line above the `Heatmap:` line.

Done when several-postings' *Done when* holds for the sample, the chat showed the `Heatmap:`, `skew:` (when printed) and `Sources skipped:` lines, and scan's records are as they were before the run (`git status` shows no change to `scan-sources.md` or a scan report).
