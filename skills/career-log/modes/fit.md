# Mode: fit

Before this mode's first step, run the **Trigger check** in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

Answer "should I go for this posting, and what am I missing?" from the log, and turn the gaps into a learning plan. It drafts nothing.

- **One posting** ("how well do I fit <URL>", "should I apply?", "what am I missing for <URL>", "how do I close the gap to <URL>"): the one-posting run, steps 1 to 7 below.
- **Several postings** ("what should I learn for these postings", "what should I learn for my targets"): the several-postings run, in [fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md).
- **Handed over by scan** (a prompt from [scan](scan.md) step 7 naming a capture file and a fit result file, with the gate, tier and things to check its Screen gave): the one-posting run, steps 1, 2, 5 and 6, with no *Trigger check* (the scan ran it). Step 1 extracts from the file, the capture, as [Capture a posting](#capture-a-posting)'s list says, and reads no page. Step 2 reads `profile.md` alone. Steps 3 and 4 are the Screen's: take its gate (a yes) and tier as given, and its things to check as theirs; step 3's sponsorship records are the scan's own (its step 7.4). Ask nothing: step 5 writes and checks the map in the fit result file. Then add to that file, in the format `python3 -I <root>/tools/fit.py --help` (`py -3 -I` on Windows) gives, the recommendation, the things to check, and as `notes` the `Course work:` and `Map:` lines the last `fit.py map` run printed; then run `python3 -I <root>/tools/fit.py --root <root> check <fit result file> --capture <capture path>` and fix what it lists until it prints `check: ok`, and stop.
- **A sponsorship question** with no posting ("does <company> sponsor H-1B?", "does <company> sponsor green cards?"): [Sponsorship lookup](#sponsorship-lookup) alone, for that company, its step 4's lines as the answer; then Next up as step 8 says, adding `--no-posting`.
- **Screen** (a prompt from [scan](scan.md) step 6 naming a batch file): the section [Screen](#screen), for each capture the batch lists.

The plan leads with projects that would become entries, unless `direction.md` says the user learns another way.

## One posting

1. **Capture the posting** as [Capture a posting](#capture-a-posting) says.
2. **Load the user's direction and profile.** Read `direction.md` and `profile.md` in full, not by search: `direction.md › Constraints` and `› Target roles, ranked` decide steps 3 and 4, and `profile.md` (education, graduation date, languages) answers requirements such as a degree.
3. **Gate.** Check each hard constraint in `direction.md › Constraints` against the posting: its authorisation language (step 1) for work authorisation, and its location, start date, level or whatever else a constraint names. A role that breaks one is a no: say so and stop, unless the user overrides it (then go on to step 4).
   - When work authorisation is empty ([SKILL.md › Setup](../SKILL.md#setup)), or Constraints says there is no constraint, there is no gate for it: list the posting's authorisation language under the recommendation as something to check, and, when it is empty, say so under the recommendation too.
   - An answer is final: when it does not mention what the posting asks (a clearance, say), list that requirement as something to check.
   - When a constraint is set and the posting says nothing about it (a user who needs H-1B sponsorship, and no authorisation language), the posting does not break it: the gate is a yes for it, and "the posting does not say whether it <the constraint's need: sponsors H-1B, say>" goes under the things to check.
   - **Student work authorisation,** while `direction.md`'s `**Visa sponsorship needed:**` line names OPT or CPT: run `python3 -I <root>/tools/sponsorship.py --root <root> wording <file>` (`py -3 -I` on Windows) on the posting's text: the capture file when there is one, else the text saved to `<root>/.build/posting.md`; in a one-posting run, also on the employer's page about work authorisation or visas (one the posting links, or the one page of its careers site a plain fetch finds), saved as text beside it. On `wording: none`, skip this bullet. Each `restricts:` line, and any other sentence there that asks for work authorisation unrestricted now and in the future, or keeps out F-1 or J-1 students or anyone on OPT or CPT, goes first under the things to check, quoted, as a requirement the user may not meet. While the line names OPT or CPT, this bullet, not the gate, judges such wording: it is never a gate no, even when work authorisation says F-1 or OPT; the recommendation is at best apply with caveats. A posting with no such sentence gets no line for it, and no line from the bullet above for saying nothing about OPT or CPT.
   - **Sponsorship records,** unless the gate is a no the user did not override: follow [Sponsorship lookup](#sponsorship-lookup) for the posting's employer, and list what its step 4 shows (or its step 1's empty-item line) under the recommendation as things to check.
4. **Tier:** where the role sits in `direction.md › Target roles, ranked`: its line's number in the list, 1 the first line, `GAP:` lines counted, so a `GAP:` line takes a number but is no role's tier. When the target roles are empty, give no tier and say so ([SKILL.md › Setup](../SKILL.md#setup)); the recommendation then rests on the gate and the requirement map.
   - A role whose title is not on the list takes a target's tier by its content: when its duties and requirements are the work a target names (a "Developer Productivity Engineer" whose duties are a "Platform engineer" target's build systems and CI), give that target's tier as `<n> (<target>, by content: <the duties that match, in the posting's words>)`. When the content matches several targets, take the one its core duties match most, the better-ranked on a tie. When it matches none, give no tier and say the role is not one of the targets; the recommendation then rests on the gate and the requirement map.
5. **Requirement map.** Follow [Requirement map](#requirement-map) whole, on step 1's requirements and duties. The conditions it takes out go under the things to check.
6. **Recommendation:** apply / apply with caveats / skip, in one line, after the gate, the tier and the map. Under it, the things to check from steps 3 and 5.
7. **Offer the next step,** both options, and stop. Offer them after a skip recommendation too, and after a gate no the user overrode: what to do next is the user's call:
   - **the resume and answers,** a `write` posting run on this posting ([write.md](write.md));
   - **a learning plan** for this posting's critical and high rows. Give every shallow, claimed-unbacked and gap row a priority from how the posting asks for it: a required skill or a core duty is critical (or high, when other requirements outweigh it), a preferred skill is high or medium, a nice-to-have is low. Then plan as [fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md) step 6 says. When the request already asked how to close the gap ("how do I close the gap to <URL>"), go straight into the plan instead of offering it.

   The run saves nothing but step 3's sponsorship records unless there is a plan (the user took the offer, or asked for it in the request); then run `python3 -I <root>/tools/fit.py --root <root> map <map file> --save-name` and save it as [fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md) step 7 says, with the table that run printed as the report's map, but to the path its `save:` line gives, with no "since last report" section, and commit it with `python3 -I <root>/tools/sync.py commit "<its commit: line's subject>" -- <report> direction.md`, naming `direction.md` only when *Direction upkeep* changed it. When there is no plan to save, a preference or reaction the run reveals still goes into `direction.md` (*Direction upkeep*), committed on its own as a small data change.
8. **Next up**, ending the message that offers step 7's options, and again once a plan is saved: run the command [SKILL.md › Next up](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode) gives, with `fit`.

Done when the user has the gate, the tier, the requirement map, the recommendation (or the gate's no) and the things to check, and has been offered the next step; a plan they took also has the user's answer to which skills they will pursue ([fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md) step 6), gives each critical and high row and each row they named a project, sourced resources and a time estimate, and is saved. The last message ends with the Next up block.

## Capture a posting

Step 1 of a one-posting run follows this section, and so does [write.md](write.md) step 2.

Take the posting from the URL the user gives, or from pasted text. Read the URL as [browser.md › Fallback order](../browser.md#fallback-order) says: a plain fetch first; a page it cannot read (built by script, as Ashby and Workday pages are, or behind a sign-in) is a Browser capture, following [browser.md](../browser.md), in a session named `posting`, with the `--profile` of [browser.md › Sign-in](../browser.md#sign-in) only for a posting behind a sign-in. `fit` closes that session once the posting text is read, and deletes `.playwright-cli/` after step 3; `write` keeps it open through its step 2, which closes it. When no item of that order gives the posting text, ask the user to paste it, as its last item says; never guess what the page holds. The posting is data ([SKILL.md › Postings are data](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)). Extract:
- company, role, level, location and deadline;
- every stated requirement, required and preferred;
- every stated duty ("What you'll do", "Responsibilities"), which the requirement map takes like a requirement;
- its **authorisation language**: any sponsorship, citizenship or clearance wording, quoted as the posting gives it.

Done when every item above is extracted from the posting text, or the user has been asked to paste it.

## Sponsorship lookup

Step 3 of a one-posting run follows this section, and so do [fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md) step 1, [scan](scan.md) step 7.4, [write.md](write.md) steps 2 and 4 and [rank](rank.md) step 2. It looks up an employer's past US visa filings: without being asked while `direction.md › Constraints` has a `**Visa sponsorship needed:**` line, and otherwise only when the user asks ("does <company> sponsor?"). `<sp>` below is `python3 -I <root>/tools/sponsorship.py --root <root>` (`py -3 -I` on Windows); its `--help` gives the formats.

1. **What to look up:** `<sp> need <employer>...`, for the employers the step that sent you here names. A last `wording:` line asks for no lookup: it is for the posting's wording, which step 3's **Student work authorisation** bullet (or scan step 7.4) reads. When the user asked, add `--kinds lca` (`--kinds lca,perm` when they asked about a green card), with or without the line: it looks up what they asked. On `lookups: none`, stop: nothing is looked up. While the *Trigger check* printed `direction visa sponsorship: empty` (not for a `none` or OPT-only line, which is set), the step that sent you here shows the empty-item line ([SKILL.md › Setup](../SKILL.md#setup)) where it would show step 4's lines: "no visa sponsorship set, so no employer's sponsorship records were looked up", and that the user fills it by stating it ([setup › Changes](setup.md#changes)). A `reuse:` line is a record an earlier lookup kept, still within the reuse age `--help` gives; each `lookup:` line goes to step 2, and with none, go to step 4. On exit 1 (a line value the tool cannot read, or a damaged records file), show its line to the user, look nothing up, and go on with the step that sent you here.
2. **Look up** each `lookup:` line's employer, by its kind. A `lookup:` line ending `unanswered <date> | <why>` was tried before and not answered: try again, starting with a source other than the one its why names. One ending `again <date>` found none under the employer's own name before. When the user gave a legal name or website for the employer, search that legal name first (for a website, the legal name its footer or terms give), then go on as **Find the employer's pages** says, its parent included.
   - `lca`: its H-1B LCA records, from sites that republish the US Department of Labor's disclosure data. For example (2026-10), a plain fetch reads `https://h1bdata.info/index.php?em=<name>&job=&city=&year=All+Years`: one page per name searched, each record a row with its filer, title, city and dates.
   - `perm`: its past PERM labor certification filings, from sites that republish the Department of Labor's disclosure data (the Department itself publishes only spreadsheets). For example (2026-10), a plain fetch reads `https://www.ellis.com/visa-sponsors/<slug>/perm`, `<slug>` the filer's legal name in lowercase words joined by hyphens (`example-robotics-inc`).

   In 2026-10, myvisajobs.com and h1bgrader.com refused a plain fetch (403): read them only as a Browser capture, after the sites above.

   **Find the employer's pages.** Search the LCA site for the employer's name first: its results give the legal names that file for it, and the PERM slug is built from one of those. When none of them is this company, find its legal name where it says it (a posting's equal-opportunity line, its site's footer or terms) and search that. A guessed slug that 404s costs a fetch. A page can be large (1 to 15 MB), and a fetch that cuts it short misses records and the newest dates: save it with `curl -sL -o <root>/.build/<name>.html "<url>"` (`curl.exe` in Windows PowerShell) and search the file, or search a narrower name.

   **Which records, and `certain`.** Keep the records filed by this company, and by its parent when a search under its own name finds none, one record per filer a page shows (a search page lists many rows under one URL):
   - The brand's own legal entity is `certain: true` when the page gives evidence that it is this company: a site on the employer's domain, or job titles and offices that match its postings. A legal name that differs from the brand (`Example Labs Inc` for the brand Example) is one, given that evidence.
   - A parent's records are `certain: false`, and the line names the parent as its `parent`, so it still says none were under the employer's own name. A sister company's records are left out.
   - A similar name the page shows for an unrelated company (`Examplex Therapeutics` beside Example) is left out. One that no page lets you tell apart from this company is `certain: false`.
   - `filer` is the name the page gives. When a site shows only a display name (the brand, not the legal name), write that, with the URL it came from.

   For each line keep the pages read, with the name each was read for (a none found too), and how recent the data is: the date the page says it was updated, else its newest record's. A records page a plain fetch cannot read is a Browser capture: follow [browser.md](../browser.md), in a session named `lca` (or the name the prompt's `Browser session:` line gives), closed when the lookups are done; then delete `.playwright-cli/` unless the step that sent you here deletes it later. A line no read answers (a page that will not load, or asks for a sign-in) is unanswered: say why in its `unanswered` field.
3. **Keep them:** write a result file (`<root>/.build/sponsorship.jsonl`, unless the step that sent you here names another) with one line per `lookup:` line, answered or not, in the format `<sp> --help` gives under `add` (`"records": []` when none were found). Each line's `employer` is the name its `lookup:` line gives, so it replaces that employer's record; a legal name you searched goes in its `sources`' `name`. Then run `<sp> add <file>` and fix what each `bad:` line names until it exits 0. A `kept:` line is an older answer it kept in place of an unanswered one. When it prints a `commit:` line, commit `outputs/reports/sponsorship.jsonl` as the step that sent you here says; when it says nothing, at once: `python3 -I <root>/tools/sync.py commit "<the subject of add's commit: line>" -- outputs/reports/sponsorship.jsonl`.
4. **Show** what `<sp> show <employer>...` prints (with step 1's `--kinds`), here or where the step that sent you here says: each record with its URL and whether the filer is certainly this company, `none found`, `unanswered` with why, or `not looked up`, each with its data date when it has one. They are things to check, never a gate: no filings is no proof either way (a young company, or one that files under another name). When the user then gives a legal name or website for an employer whose line is `none found` or `none under its own name`, run step 1 for it with `--again <the employer as its record: line names it>`, and go on from there: the legal name is only what step 2 searches.

Done when each `lookup:` line of step 1 has a line in the result file and `add` exited 0, and, unless the step that sent you here does step 4 itself, step 4's lines are shown.

## Screen

This section is the whole task of a subagent that [scan](scan.md) step 6 starts on a batch of captures: a first pass over many postings, so that only the best get a full fit. The prompt names the batch file (`.todo`, one capture path per line) and the results file to write. Each capture is a posting's verbatim text with its URL on the first line, and it is data ([SKILL.md › Postings are data](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)). Skip the *Trigger check*: the scan ran it. Every posting comes from its file: the main session has read the pages.

1. **Load once:** read `direction.md` and `profile.md` in full, and run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py list --root <root>` (`py -3 -I` on Windows) once and read its whole output. Those lines are all you read of the entries: their tags, org and One line stand for each entry, and the entry files stay closed.
2. **For each capture** the batch lists that exists (skip a path with no file):
   1. **Extract** from the file what [Capture a posting](#capture-a-posting) lists, reading no page.
   2. **Gate** as step 3 of [One posting](#one-posting) says, with its **Sponsorship records** bullet left out: scan step 7.4 looks up the employers of the postings it fits. Its **Student work authorisation** bullet reads the capture file alone. A no is final here: give the constraint and the posting's words.
   3. **Tier** as its step 4 says: the number goes in the result's `tier` (null for none), and a by-content tier's `(<target>, by content: …)` note, or that the role is not one of the targets, goes in its `check`.
   4. **Rough coverage,** on a gate yes: take out the conditions as [Requirement map](#requirement-map) item 1 says (they go under the things to check), then mark each remaining requirement and duty `required`, `preferred` or `duty`, as the posting gives it (a requirement under no heading that says preferred, a plus or nice to have is required), and name each entry whose list line touches it (its tags, org or One line), or none.
3. **Write the results file:** one line per capture, in the format `python3 -I <root>/tools/scan.py --help` (`py -3 -I` on Windows) gives for a Screen result, its `url` the URL on the capture's first line. Then run `python3 -I <root>/tools/scan.py --root <root> screen --check <results file> --batch <batch file>`, and fix what it lists until it prints `check: ok`.

Done when `screen --check` prints `check: ok` for the batch, and each gate yes has one row per requirement and duty that is not a condition.

## Select entries

Item 3 of [Requirement map](#requirement-map) follows this section, and so does [write.md](write.md) step 5 in a target run. Its input is what the entries must evidence: the requirements and duties, or in a target run each skill or theme `direction.md › What to emphasise` names for the target (the target's role name when it names none).

1. **List the entries.** Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py list --root <root>` once in the run and read its whole output, not a search of it: one line per entry, with its series, type, org, period, tags and One line. Read `stories.md` too.
2. **Select from the list:** every entry whose tags, One line or org, or a `stories.md` row pointing at it, touches the input.
3. **Search the rest.** For each item of the input no selected entry touches yet, search the bodies of the entries the list names and step 2 did not select for its key terms: the technology, domain or tool it names, in the input's words and in the record language. Select each entry that matches. Tags are incomplete, so an untagged experience is found here.
4. **A series is one experience.** A selected entry whose list line shows a series other than `-` is one part of it: select every line with the same series. Read all its parts and cite each fact by the part that holds it. [write.md](write.md) step 6 says how an output writes a series.
5. **Read each selected entry whole:** one read of the whole file, with no line range, offset or limit, and no filter such as `sed -n`, `head` or `cut`, so `## My contribution`, `## Past phrasings`, `## Conflicts` with its `Settled:` lines, and `## Pending` are all read.

Done when the list was read whole, every item of the input either touches a selected entry or had its key terms searched in the unselected entries, every part of each selected series is selected, and every selected entry was read whole in this run.

## Requirement map

Step 5 of a one-posting run follows this section, and so do [fit-several-postings.md](fit-several-postings.md) step 3 and [write.md](write.md) step 5. Its input is the requirements and duties the step that sent you here gives. The map is a map file, one per posting, in the format `python3 -I <root>/tools/fit.py --help` (`py -3 -I` on Windows) gives, at the path, relative to `<root>`, that `python3 -I <root>/tools/fit.py --root <root> name <company> <role>` prints on its `map file:` line (a fit result file handed over by scan is its map file).

1. **Take out the conditions.** What is neither a skill nor a duty (a background check, a location, a pay range, a start date, years of experience) gets no row; the step that sent you here says where it goes. Every skill and every duty the posting names stays a row, including one the log never touches, which is a `gap` row.
2. **List the course-work entries:** run [Select entries](#select-entries) step 1 now, with `--course-work` added to its command (item 3 reuses its output). Take each entry its `course work:` line names, and, from every line of the list, not only the entries you read, each entry whose org names a course. A capstone (an `org` or a tag that names one) is not course work: judge its parts by the values below, as for any project, with teammates' parts as `collaborator`.
3. **Select and read the entries** as [Select entries](#select-entries) says, on this map's requirements and duties. Each entry a row cites is one of them, read whole in this run.
4. **Tie each row to one entry,** the one that evidences it. When several entries back a row, weigh each by the rules below and cite the one that gives the strongest category. Mark each row's `kind` as the posting gives it. A requirement that names several skills ("Go, Python, SQL") becomes one row per skill when they would fall in different categories. An either-or requirement ("Go or Python", "a language such as Go or Rust", "one of Java, C#") stays one row, since one alternative meets it: on the alternative whose evidence gives the strongest category, named alone as its `requirement` (and its `skill`, where the step that sent you here asks for one), with its `of` the number of its `requirements` item. When every alternative would be a `gap`, the one row is that `gap`, named as the posting names it ("Go or Python"), in its `requirement` and its `skill`.
5. **Fill in "written by"** before the category: one value per row, from the one entry the row cites. A row that rests only on the entry's front matter `tags` (item 8, **shallow**) is `unknown`, whatever the list below gives. When different people or agents wrote the sections of that entry that touch the row (one phase by a collaborator, another by an agent), take the section that gives the strongest category (item 4's rule), name it beside the entry's file name in the map ("`2026-01-x.md` (phase 3)"), and give that section's value alone. A series part is its own entry: cite the part's file. The value is the first of these that applies:
   1. `coursework`: the entry is on the course-work list, even when the user wrote every line;
   2. `collaborator`: another person (not an AI agent) wrote this section, even when that person worked through an agent, including work under *Others did; I can explain it* or a `Settled:` line that gives it to another person;
   3. `AI agent`: the entry's `## My contribution` or a `Settled:` line says an agent wrote the code for this section, even under the user's direction or *I led, with help from*;
   4. `unknown`: the entry leaves open who wrote this section (a `GAP:` on it);
   5. `user`: the user wrote it.
6. **Fill in "own work"** for an `AI agent` row: a label, then the line it quotes (whole words, three at least), or `none`. The line comes word for word from the entry's `## My contribution`, `Settled:` lines or `## How it works`, is about this section, and has the user as the one acting: a line under **I did:**, or one where the user does the work ("caught that …", "chose …"). A line where someone else found or checked it ("had the code checked"), or where the user only reported a symptom or handed off the whole problem ("had it fixed", "asked the agent to fix the failing test"), makes the cell `none`. The label is one of three, and nothing else counts:
   - `design:` the user's own design decision: an architecture, an interface, a tech choice, including a plan or a discussion in which the user picked one;
   - `debugging:` the user found what broke and why; the fix itself can be the agent's ("traced the drift to X and had the agent fix it");
   - `review:` the user read the agent's code or output and corrected it.

   A line of any other kind (testing by hand, scoring, approving, scheduling or splitting up the work, learning from an explanation) makes the cell `none`. Two kinds of row take only some labels (the map file's `row_type`):
   - a row naming a language or a tool ("Go", "TypeScript", "Kubernetes"): `debugging:` or `review:`, with the line showing the user in that code itself;
   - a row for debugging or diagnosing: `debugging:` only.

   On those rows, a line with another label (a `design:` line, such as choosing between fixes) leaves the row at most shallow.
7. **Cap the row** by its value:
   - `user`: by the depth the entry shows;
   - `AI agent`: covered only with a labelled, quoted "own work" line that counts for this row, whatever the depth the posting asks; with `none`, at most shallow;
   - `unknown`: at most shallow;
   - `collaborator`: at most shallow when the posting asks for build depth, however much of the work there is;
   - `coursework`: at most shallow, unless the requirement asks only for understanding, knowledge, exposure or familiarity ("deep understanding of distributed systems", "familiarity with compilers"; `row_type` `understanding`): then covered when the entry shows it.
8. **Sort** each row into **exactly one** of these four categories, and no other label:
   - **covered:** an entry shows the user doing it at the depth the posting asks ("operate" vs "build"), and the row's cap allows it;
   - **shallow:** an entry shows it, but below the depth asked (used, not built), or the row's cap holds it there; or an entry's front matter `tags` name it and no line of that entry shows the work: cite that entry, and put "`<entry>` tags <skill>, but no line says what the work was: ask the user" under the things to check, where item 1's conditions go;
   - **claimed, unbacked:** on a resume or in `profile.md` with no entry behind it;
   - **gap:** nowhere in the log (a skill only an entry's `tags` name is **shallow**), including a skill the log cannot show (communication, problem solving), which the interview judges.

   Name who built what as the entry's `## My contribution` and its `Settled:` lines say: a collaborator's build is never the user's, even where a resume or an imported document once said so.
9. **Run the map check** once every row has its category: write the map file, then run `python3 -I <root>/tools/fit.py --root <root> map <map file>` and fix what its `bad:` lines list until it prints `map: ok`. A requirement or duty its `Map:` line names under `no row:` gets a `gap` row when the log never touches it. With several postings, give it every posting's map file in one run, with `--several`.
10. **Show the map:** put the `Course work:` line, the `Map:` line and the table that the last run of `fit.py map` printed into your reply, as it printed them: the user may not see a tool's output. Show every row, however many; a count or a summary by category is not the map.

Done when every map file's `requirements` holds each requirement and duty of the input that is not a condition, `fit.py map` printed `map: ok` for every map file, every cited entry was read whole in this run, and, unless the step that sent you here skips or defers **Show the map**, the map is shown as that item says.
