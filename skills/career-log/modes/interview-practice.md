# Interview practice: rounds

Read this when the interview mode's Diagnose, Practise or Final review phase starts ([interview.md](interview.md)). Practice is a run of short **rounds**: one item asked, the answer heard, checked against the Log, given a verdict, written down, and retested while it is weak. `<folder>` is the application folder and `<stage>` the prep file's stage, as in interview.md. Before building a page for practice (a teaching page, a rehearsal page, a cue card), read [pages.md](../pages.md).

## A round

1. **Pick the item.** One item of the prep file or a sub-item under it: a technical topic (`areas/<topic>`), a Story (`story/<key>`), a short answer (`tell-me-about-yourself`, `why-this-company`, `awkward-questions`), the talk or one of its parts (`talk/<part>`), or a Story followed by technical follow-ups drawn from what it describes (logged under `story/<key>`). The user picks, or take the first `retest:` line `prep-status` prints, then the schedule's line for today, then the first area, Story or short answer with no attempt in the practice log. `practice` and `mock` are never a round's item.
2. **Ask it with its bar.** The question asks for what the item's bar wants, so an answer without it cannot pass: "…and tie it to something you built", "…what did you do, not what would you do", "…in under two minutes". The bar is the item's done bar from the Plan phase, the rules of [speaking.md](../speaking.md) for a behavioural answer, and any pattern already in the practice log's `## Patterns`. Before a technical item, ask whether any term in it is unclear, and teach from where the user is before asking. A side question about a term ("what is X?") belongs to the round: answer it there; when the user asked it in another session, ask what it was once the round ends. A side question that shows a gap (a term the item's bar takes for granted) becomes one bullet under the practice log's `## Patterns`, naming the item.
3. **Hear it** the way the interview will hear it. Typed; spoken and recorded, then transcribed as below; live, the user answering turn by turn; or a pasted transcript of a round held elsewhere (*A round held elsewhere* below). An item the interview will hear spoken passes only after one spoken round; a design answer is also drawn (on paper or a whiteboard, the user describing or photographing it), and, when the posting or the research says a listener outside the field will be there, explained for that listener once. A recording is transcribed:
   - **with speech.py**, when the check printed `speech: ok`: `python3 -I <root>/tools/speech.py transcribe <recording>`, adding `--terms "<names>"` with the item's own proper nouns (the products, companies, projects and tools in its entry, its prep file item and, for the talk, `talk.md`), as one comma-separated string; `--parts <cue file>` for a timed run of the talk or of some of its parts, the cue file written to a temporary folder outside the Log and deleted after the round, one line per part the run covers, in order, each that part's first words in `talk.md`'s `## Script`; and `--check` when the item's bar checks facts, numbers or terms against the Log or the script (such as a Story or the talk). Read the answer's length from `duration:`'s speech span (`speech <start> to <end>`), the pace from `wpm:`, the pauses from `pauses:`, each part's time from its `part:` line (against its window in `talk.md`'s `## Outline`; for a part printed `not found`, ask the user where it started), and quote the answer from the text after `transcript:` (a `dropped:` line is the model's, never the user's words). Each `heard differently:` stretch is asked about before it counts against the answer: the user says what they said there, and an unclear word is a fix to say it more clearly.
   - **else**, when the check's `next:` line for a spoken answer has run and the check, run again, still prints no `speech: ok`: by the user's own app or this app's voice input, the user pasting or dictating the text.
4. **Feedback**, in the conversation language ([SKILL.md › Languages](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)), in this order:
   1. the verdict: `passed`, `near`, `shaky` or `again`;
   2. what landed;
   3. what to fix, each point against its source: the item's done bar; for a behavioural answer, each rule under speaking.md's *Telling a Story* and *Length and follow-ups* it misses, by number; or the entry's facts;
   4. each statement the Log does not hold or contradicts, quoted, with the entry it was checked against, asked as a question: is it a fact the entry lacks, or a slip? A fact goes into its entry's section with `(user, YYYY-MM-DD)` in this session; a slip goes under the practice log's `## Do not say` with its correction;
   5. a rewrite of the answer in the user's own words and register (`direction.md`), in the answer's output language, keeping what landed.
5. **Write the verdict** before the next round: `python3 -I <root>/tools/application.py practice-add <folder> --stage <stage> --root <root> --item <item> --verdict <verdict> --kind <kind> --weak "<what to fix, short>" --heard <typed|recorded|live>`, with `--kind diagnostic` for a Diagnose round, `retest` for a retest, `mock` for a mock's rounds, `drill` for a technical topic's study round ([interview-technical.md › Study](interview-technical.md#study), which adds its own steps to this round), else left out. Rows for one item in one session (a Story's telling and its follow-ups, all `story/<key>`) go in with the weakest verdict last, since `prep-status` reads an item's latest row for `retest:`. A fault seen in a second item goes under `## Patterns` as one bullet. Commit the log (and any entry that took a fact): `python3 -I <root>/tools/sync.py commit "interview(<company>): <stage> practice" -- <file>...`.

## Retests

An item whose latest verdict is `shaky` or `again` prints as `retest:` on `prep-status`. Ask it once more, with the same bar, on a later day than its last attempt when the schedule has one, and always before the Final review ends, unless the user drops it there; its new verdict replaces the old in `prep-status`. Offer the retests before new items each session.

## A round held elsewhere

A transcript the user brings back from a round held outside the session (a voice app, a friend asking, a recording of the user alone):
- **Only the user's words are evidence.** In a transcript with two voices, the other voice's lines give the question; the feedback quotes, checks and judges the user's lines only.
- **Several answers in one transcript** are matched to their items by the prep file's item names (a sub-item when one fits), and each gets a round of its own from step 4: its own feedback, its own verdict and its own `practice-add` row, `--heard recorded`.
- **The way back out.** The feedback ends with a short prompt the user can take back to the outside round: the items that came out `shaky` or `again`, each as the question to ask with its bar, in the answer's output language.
- **When to record and when to go live.** Timed runs of the talk and a Story's first telling go by recording, since timing and pace need the whole answer. Follow-up questions and a mock go live in the session, since a recording costs minutes per turn.

## Diagnose, mock and Final review

- **Diagnose** is one round per area (each technical area, the behavioural questions, the talk), `--kind diagnostic`, before the Plan phase sizes the effort: timed, its feedback kept to the verdict and what fell short.
- **A full mock** is rounds strung together in the stage's order: a warm-up, then questions from the pack one at a time, each heard live, then a curveball. Offer it once every area has a `passed` round; it is optional. Feedback follows step 4 after each answer, kept to its verdict and one fix so the mock keeps moving; the full feedback for each answer comes after the curveball, then a `practice-add` row per answer, `--kind mock`.
- **Final review** ([interview-day.md › Final review](interview-day.md#final-review)) asks each `retest:` item the user keeps once more, as a round with *Retests*' bar; its technical card carries the practice log's `## Do not say` list.

Done when every round asked has its verdict in the practice log, each statement the Log did not hold went into its entry or under `## Do not say`, each round brought back from elsewhere ended with the retest prompt, and the practice log and any entry it changed are committed.
