# Mode: consolidate

Before this mode's first step, run the **Trigger check** in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

Turn an entry's pending lines, links and imported phrasings into a record of what an output can use. A detail **belongs** in the entry when a resume line, an interview answer, or the first follow-up on either (why this way, how it works, what went wrong, what you would change) would use it, or when it supports one. Anything else is a link to where it lives, and a deeper follow-up reaches it through that link, as [interview](interview.md)'s deep-dive notes do. Details that typically become links: commit SHAs, line and word counts, the head each platform was tested on, a script's output format, container paths, a feature's step-by-step history, a per-workflow list of CI jobs. Every section, `## How it works`, `## Decisions and trade-offs` and `## Dead ends and mistakes` included, keeps what belongs, whatever its length. Every run applies this mode's rules to the whole entry, so an older entry adopts them at its next consolidate.

1. **Read what is new.** The entry itself and every link `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py links-since <entry> --root <root>` prints. Pull out facts, numbers, dates, decisions, dead ends and review comments, each with its provenance. A source the entry's sections already cite was read by an earlier run: open it again only to verify a fact this run changes or a conflict disputes.
2. **Draft every section of the template** from that material. Where the material cannot answer, write `GAP:` with the specific question. When filling or rewriting a section, a sourced fact, attribution lines above all, moves into the new section with its source. It leaves the entry only when the user or newer evidence says it is wrong, or as the bullets below prescribe (condensing, placed pending lines, trimmed past phrasings, a settled conflict's list of versions).
   - **Condense.** Delete only these three:
     - superseded state (an earlier design, a replaced component, an older measurement): the earlier state stays as one sentence in `## Decisions and trade-offs` or `## Dead ends and mistakes`, and only when it belongs;
     - a detail that does not belong, once its source is durable ([SKILL.md › Provenance](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)): its link stays where the detail was. A detail that cites a session is excerpted first ([../excerpts.md](../excerpts.md)), and the link is the excerpt's key. A detail cited `(user, YYYY-MM-DD)` alone is excerpted first too (search `find` with words the user would have typed, in their language; a quote whose transcript is gone is copied by hand, as excerpts.md says). When no excerpt can hold the user's words (a paraphrase whose turn `find` cannot locate), the detail stays;
     - a duplicate: keep it once, in the section it fits best.

     Merge the lines about one thread into one sentence that carries all their sources. Each merge stays inside one attribution category (*I did*, *I led, with help from*, *Others did*) and one side of a conflict; `Settled:` lines stay as they are. A newer measurement replaces an older one in place; the older stays only to state a change, in one sentence. `## How it works` states the current design; a decision that shaped it goes in Decisions when it belongs.
   - **Excerpt the user's words.** A quote of the user's words, with or without a bracketed translation, becomes a paraphrase in the record language. Its original goes into an excerpt ([../excerpts.md](../excerpts.md), whose `find` locates the transcript), unless the fact already cites a file in `sources/` that holds it, and the fact keeps its `(user, YYYY-MM-DD)` and gains the excerpt's key. A fact that cites a session any other way than by an excerpt key gets its excerpt the same way. The paraphrase then takes the belongs test like any other detail: one that belongs stays with its key, and one that does not is deleted under *Condense*, its key staying as the link.
   - **Place every pending line** in the section it belongs to, then delete it. A line's leading date is the day it was recorded: it becomes "as of <date>" on a number a later measurement can replace (and that later measurement replaces it in place), and disappears otherwise, except as the source date of a fact the user stated (`(user, YYYY-MM-DD)`). A date for an event goes into the body only when evidence gives it, and only where an output could use it (the `period`, a launch month).
   - **Settle conflicts the material or the user resolves.** Write the right value in its section with its source, and collapse the conflict to one `Settled:` line in one of the forms `entries/_template.md` gives under `## Conflicts`: the do-not-use form, for a claim with no support, has a quoted claim and a why-clause that name the false claim itself, not the technique or topic it mentions ([../writing.md](../writing.md) › Principles 3). Drop the list of versions; the sources keep them. Evidence settles what is true, including that both sides are (the both-right form); a pick between competing true wordings, or a conflict an approved output merely sidestepped, is the user's ([SKILL.md › Current truth only](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)). Every other conflict stays as a line starting `Open:` (add the marker to an older line that lacks it).
   - **Trim `## Past phrasings`** to at most two wordings per claim per angle (the claim and the angle as `entries/_template.md` defines them). Of near-identical wordings keep one. Rank a wording from an approved output above an imported one, then the newer above the older, and keep the top two. Each keeps one citation; delete anything else there: usage notes, extra citations, and the dates, companies or postings it was used for outside the citation. Names inside the verbatim wording stay.
   - **Keep `links:` to repo roots and main sites**, one each, as its comment in `entries/_template.md` says. Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py check --consolidated <entry> --root <root>`: the URL of each `link not a repo root:` line it prints moves into the body as the citation of the fact it supports, or is deleted when no fact cites it, and its root is added to `links:` when the line says `missing from links`; so does any other page in `links:` that is not a main site (the check reads GitHub and GitLab URLs only). Its other lines are for this mode's Done when. A root stays even when the body cites it: `tools/deny_coverage.py` reads names from `links:` only.
   - **Make `tags` cover the body:** every main technology, tool, domain and competency the body shows the user using gets a tag, in the form the other entries already use. fit and write select entries from the tags `tools/entries.py list` prints ([fit.md › Select entries](fit.md#select-entries)).
   - **Re-cite facts that cite an output** (a resume, report or answer; the `check --consolidated` run in the `links:` bullet names them on its `check: output citation:` lines) with the source in that output's provenance table; when the output has since been deleted, read its table at the cited commit (`git -C <root> show <sha>:<path>`).
   - **Deny the names the front matter records.** Whenever this run creates an entry file or changes an entry's `org`, `title` or `links:` (in this step, a split at step 4 or a correction at step 5), deny the names of each entry file this run created or whose front matter it changed, as [commits.md › Names appended to `.export-deny`](../commits.md#names-appended-to-export-deny) says, without waiting for step 5's approval.
3. **Interview the user on the gaps**, in rounds. Each round asks every open question that does not depend on another open answer, numbered, with a recommended answer the user can accept or correct. A recommended answer comes only from the log: a sourced fact, or a reading of one marked `(interpretation, not stated)`, with its source named. For a fact only the user knows (who did which part, who the others were, why a decision went one way), the recommended answer is "unknown": never a plausible guess, because a guess the user accepts becomes a sourced fact. Ask every one of the question types below that the entry has a gap for, not only the first few. Prioritise in this order:
   - attribution (what exactly did *I* do versus others or tools),
   - visibility, when the front matter says `visibility: GAP`: public, private or nda ([SKILL.md › Visibility](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)). Write the answer with `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py set-visibility <entry> <answer> --root <root>`,
   - open conflicts: which side is right,
   - the reasons behind decisions and the alternatives rejected,
   - people: feedback received, disagreements, who was helped,
   - what was learned and what they would do differently,
   - the questions an interviewer is most likely to ask, with draft answers.
   Record each answer with `(user, YYYY-MM-DD)` in the section it belongs to; an answer that sets one of the front matter fields *Provenance* in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode) names cites it as the field's comment. An unanswerable question stays a `GAP:` with a note. Record "user does not remember" only for a question the user was asked and answered that way; a question not asked stays a plain `GAP:`. If the user says the experience did not happen or does not count, follow *Dropped experiences* in SKILL.md instead of finishing the entry.
4. **Split an entry still over the threshold.** Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py check --root <root>` on the draft. Only an `entry over threshold:` line naming this entry matters here; with none, go to step 5. The belongs test alone decides each deletion, so an entry holding only what belongs and still over the threshold covers more than one phase, and the remedy is a split, never a deeper cut. Propose the split and wait for the user's reply:
   - **The unit** is what a reader picks for one posting: a project within a long job, a phase within a long project. Cut where the goal, the role or the scope changed, on a month the evidence gives; when no evidence dates a cut, ask the user for its month in the same message. A cut no one can date is not made: propose another, or keep the entry whole.
   - **For each part,** give its file name, title, period, `## One line` and the sections it takes. Each part starts where the one before it ends, unless the evidence shows the work paused between them: then the earlier part ends when the pause began. A part that would still be over the threshold gets a further cut in the same proposal.

   When the user says no, go to step 5 with the one entry. On their yes to the cut points, or to the ones they corrected, write the parts:
   - **Files.** Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py split <entry> --phase "<the earliest part's phase>" --cut "<YYYY-MM[-DD]>:<part slug>:<phase>" --root <root>`, one `--cut` per later part (its approved start, a short slug naming its phase, the phase; `<YYYY-MM[-DD]>..` before the start when the work paused between the two parts, the earlier part's end), and follow its output.
   - **Each part stands alone:** its own `## One line`, `## Context`, `## My contribution` with all three categories, tags and `last_updated`, and the sections its facts fill, with `org`, `visibility` and `links:` the same in every part; a finished part's `## How it works` states the design as that phase left it. A fact two parts need is written in each, in the words that part needs.
   - **Move, not condense.** Every fact, `## Past phrasings` line and `## Conflicts` line goes with its source into the part it is about, placed by the dates and citations the entry already holds (step 1's rule on reopening sources holds here too); a `Settled:` line goes into every part that holds the claim it rules on.
   - **References.** Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py refs <entry> --root <root>`, and point each line in `stories.md` and `profile.md` it lists at the part that now holds what the line names. Each Talking points file a `talking points:` line names goes on step 6's list with a **part note**: each of its lines `refs` lists and the part that now holds what the line names. Its content is the user's to confirm, so it stays as it is. Leave lines in application folders, `sources/` and other files as they are: those are frozen or historical, and the earliest part kept the entry's path, so the path they name still resolves.

   Run `check` again: done when it prints no `series` finding and no README finding for any part, and no `entry over threshold:` line for any part, unless the user kept that part whole.
5. **Show the user the entry's changes.** Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py diff <entry> [<part>…] --root <root>`, every part named after a split (the updated entry alone hides what was deleted and merged). Running the command shows its output only to you, so each time this step shows the changes (after a correction or a rejected split too), your message holds, in this order (on `no change:`, say the entry is unchanged):
   - **The change list:** paste the lines the `paste:` line names (the `changes` block, fences included) into your reply whole, every line as printed, however many: the tool builds the list from the diff, so it holds every deletion, and the user reviews each line in the chat.
   - **The rules,** right after the block: for each `deleted:` and `changed:` line, by its `<file>:<line>`, the *Condense* rule it follows (lines that follow one rule may share its item); a detail moved into an excerpt names the excerpt, and the lines of one merge say they were merged. After a split, each `moved:` line already names its part; a fact, `## Past phrasings` line or `## Conflicts` line reworded on its way to a part is a `deleted:` line, so its item names the part it went to. For each file under an `other Log file:` heading, one item says why its lines changed (in a bulk run, an entry an earlier step 5 showed is listed again: say so). Put back as it was each file under a `not consolidate's to change:` heading that this run wrote, then run `diff` again before you show the list. A change there that this run did not write stays, and its item says it was there before the run.
   - Each `size:` line.
   - The `diff file:` path and its `open:` command: the full diff, to read in an editor.
   - **The block**, when `diff` prints one (one entry and a small change), pasted verbatim, its `[… N words unchanged …]` marks included. When the user asks to see the diff in the chat, run `diff` again with `--paste` and paste its block.
   - The new excerpts.

   A bulk run shows each entry's changes at that entry's step 5; the full-diff read that [commits.md › Bulk data changes](../commits.md#bulk-data-changes) asks for comes later, before the bulk yes. Run `diff` after your last edit to the entry, so what you show is the entry as step 7 commits it; then stop and wait for their reply. Their yes covers the changes in that message only: an edit after it (a correction, a reworded line) is shown the same way and waits for its own yes. When they reject the split itself, put the parts' content back in the entry, then run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py split --undo <entry> <part>… --root <root>`, point each line its `still names` lines list back at the entry, and do what each `next:` line says; take the files step 4 put on step 6's list, with their part notes, off it (step 6 lists them again from the restored entry), and show the one entry again. Apply their corrections to everything this step shows and to the part notes, and show the result again if they changed anything. The diff is **approved** when the user says yes to the last version shown, so each correction restarts the wait. A change to an approved entry, at a bulk change's yes included, sends it back through this step. Everything that describes the result, apart from what this step shows the user (after a split, step 4's README rows and pointers), waits for that approval: its size or `check` numbers, a README note, a line in another file's `## Pending`, a report, a follow-up or issue, and the commit (steps 6 and 7 start only after it). Write each from the approved files, and take any size or `check` number from a new run on them: step 4's run on the draft only decides the split.
6. **Update the indexes and list Talking points.** `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py set-status <entry> consolidated --root <root>`, or `"consolidated (<short note>)"` when gaps remain, which writes the status in the entry's front matter, then `README.md`'s Entries table whole from every entry's front matter, so a title or period this run changed shows too; after a split, once for each part. Then `stories.md`:
   - **Finish raw and pending Stories.** Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py story-finish <entry> --root <root>` on this entry, or on each part after a split. It drops the markers `raw` and `pending` from each item that links the entry at one of its sections, and prints each item for you to repoint or leave:
     - `links no section:` an item marked `raw` or `pending` that links the entry with no section, or with one the entry no longer has, or that links `#pending`. Repoint it, or delete it as below.
     - `raw source:` an item marked `raw` that links a file in `sources/` the entry cites. Repoint it only when the story its label and summary name is this entry's (any part, after a split); leave another entry's as it is. The tool finds a source only by the path the entry writes, so read the index too for a `raw` item that links a source the entry cites by its key, and treat it the same way.

     Each item you repoint now links the section that holds its facts, in whichever part holds them (the section with the user's actions, when they are spread over several), labelled `<that part's title> › <section>`; its markers and summary stay as they are. When the row already holds that link, merge the two at the earlier item's place, keeping both items' markers and the existing item's summary (the repointed one's when only it has one). When no section holds its facts any longer (step 2 deleted them or made them a link), delete the item; if it was the experience's only `core` item, move `core` to another of its items, or, with none left, say that it is no longer core. Then run `story-finish` again: it drops the markers from the items you repointed.
   - **Add this entry** (each part, after a split) under every behavioural question type it can answer, one `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py story-add "<question type>" <entry>#<section anchor> --summary "<one line>" --root <root>` per section that answers it, with the anchor of that section: its heading lowercased, spaces as `-`, and every character other than a letter, digit, `-` or `_` dropped (`## Decisions and trade-offs` is `#decisions-and-trade-offs`); `already linked:` means the row has it. Then run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py story-coverage --root <root>`: a `story under too many types:` line for a link this step added or repointed goes to the user with the types and your proposal of which to leave out; wait for their reply and take out the ones they leave out before step 7. An item you repoint by hand is in the Story index format of the docstring at the top of `tools/entries.py` (*Stories*), with its escapes.

   Then **list the Talking points files** that point at this entry: run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py refs <entry> --root <root>` on this entry (on each part, after a split); each `talking points:` line names one, with its question type, and `links #pending` when it links the entry's `#pending` (which now leads to an empty section). A file already on the list from step 4 stays there once, with its part note. Tell the user each file, with its question type, any `#pending` link, and that the [stories](stories.md) mode reconfirms it; with no `talking points:` line, say in one line that no Talking points point at it. The files stay as they are: their content is the user's to confirm.

   Done when the entry's status (each part's) is written and its last `readme` line printed, the last `story-finish` run on this entry, or on each part after a split, printed `no story index:` or no `links no section:` line, and each `raw source:` line it printed names an item another entry holds, every type this entry answers links it (except those the user left out), and the user has answered any `story under too many types:` line for a link this step wrote, been told each deleted item with its question type and the list of Talking points files that point at it, or that there are none.
7. **Commit and push**, only after step 5: `python3 -I <root>/tools/sync.py commit "consolidate(<slugs>): <summary>" -- <file>...`, naming the entry (every part, after a split), the indexes and the new excerpts, where `<slugs>` is the slug of every entry file the commit changes (its file name without `<YYYY-MM>-` and `.md`), comma-separated: `links-since` finds an entry's last consolidate by its own slug, a split's later parts and a bulk run's entries included. When one request consolidates more than one entry, it is a bulk change: commit once every entry has been through step 5, on the user's yes, as [commits.md › Bulk data changes](../commits.md#bulk-data-changes) says.
8. **Resume-ready check,** on a `type: project` entry only (each part, after a split): in the last message, before the Next up block, list which of these the entry still lacks, read from its sections and `links:`: the problem (`## Context`), the named stack with a reason for each (`## How it works`), a measured result with its baseline and method (`## Results and evidence`), a reproducible setup (a repo or the steps to rerun it), a demo, one trade-off with the alternative it rejected (`## Decisions and trade-offs`), and a write-up (the demo and the write-up: a link in `## Results and evidence` or `links:`). With none missing, say it in one line. This is advice for the project, not missing information: write no `GAP:` for it, and it does not hold up Done when.
9. **Next up**, after step 7's push and step 8's list: run the command [SKILL.md › Next up](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode) gives, with `consolidate`.

Done when every section is filled or carries an explicit `GAP:` the user could not answer, every detail belongs or is a link, every quote of the user is replaced by a paraphrase citing its excerpt (a `(user, YYYY-MM-DD)` fact without a key is one step 3 recorded or one whose words no excerpt can hold), every deletion and merge appeared in step 5's list with its *Condense* rule (and, after a split, every moved line with its part), an entry step 4 found over the threshold is split as the user approved or kept whole on their no, `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py check --consolidated <entry>... --root <root>`, with every entry file this run wrote, prints `OK:`, or no finding but `entry over threshold:` lines for entries the user kept whole (each `check: output citation:` line re-cited as step 2 says; each `check: record note:` line deleted when it is a note about the record, kept when it is a fact about the work), `links:` holds only repo roots and main sites, one each, `## Past phrasings` holds at most two wordings per claim per angle, every number has its measurement (for a gain, its baseline, in `How measured`) and source, and `stories.md` points at this entry (each part, after a split) for every story it holds, step 6's own Done-when holds, and the change is committed and pushed (for a bulk change the user said no to: undone). The last message follows [SKILL.md › Chat output](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode) and ends with the Next up block.
