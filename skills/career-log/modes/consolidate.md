# Mode: consolidate

Before this mode's first step, run the **Start check** in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), once per session.

Turn an entry's pending lines, links and imported phrasings into a record of what an output can use. A detail **belongs** in the entry when a resume line, an interview answer, or the first follow-up on either (why this way, how it works, what went wrong, what you would change) would use it, or when it supports one. Anything else is a link to where it lives, and a deeper follow-up reaches it through that link, as [interview](interview.md)'s deep-dive notes do. Details that typically become links: commit SHAs, line and word counts, the head each platform was tested on, a script's output format, container paths, a feature's step-by-step history, a per-workflow list of CI jobs. Every section, `## How it works`, `## Decisions and trade-offs` and `## Dead ends and mistakes` included, keeps what belongs, whatever its length. Every run applies this mode's rules to the whole entry, so an older entry adopts them at its next consolidate.

1. **Read what is new.** The entry itself; the links in its `## Pending`, `## Past phrasings` and `## Conflicts`; and the links `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py links-since <entry> <root>` prints, which are those added since the entry's last consolidate (all of them for an entry never consolidated). Pull out facts, numbers, dates, decisions, dead ends and review comments, each with its provenance. A source the entry's sections already cite was read by an earlier run: open it again only to verify a fact this run changes or a conflict disputes.
2. **Draft every section of the template** from that material. Where the material cannot answer, write `GAP:` with the specific question. When filling or rewriting a section, a sourced fact, attribution lines above all, moves into the new section with its source. It leaves the entry only when the user or newer evidence says it is wrong, or as the bullets below prescribe (condensing, placed pending lines, trimmed past phrasings, a settled conflict's list of versions).
   - **Condense.** Delete only these three:
     - superseded state (an earlier design, a replaced component, an older measurement): the earlier state stays as one sentence in `## Decisions and trade-offs` or `## Dead ends and mistakes`, and only when it belongs;
     - a detail that does not belong, once its source is durable ([SKILL.md › Provenance](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)): its link stays where the detail was. A detail that cites a session is excerpted first ([../excerpts.md](../excerpts.md)), and the link is the excerpt's key. A detail cited `(user, YYYY-MM-DD)` alone is excerpted first too (search `find` with words the user would have typed, in their language; a quote whose transcript is gone is copied by hand, as excerpts.md says). When no excerpt can hold the user's words (a paraphrase whose turn `find` cannot locate), the detail stays;
     - a duplicate: keep it once, in the section it fits best.

     Merge the lines about one thread into one sentence that carries all their sources. Each merge stays inside one attribution category (*I did*, *I led, with help from*, *Others did*) and one side of a conflict; `Settled:` lines stay as they are. A newer measurement replaces an older one in place; the older stays only to state a change, in one sentence. `## How it works` states the current design; a decision that shaped it goes in Decisions when it belongs.
   - **Excerpt the user's words.** A quote of the user's words, with or without a bracketed translation, becomes a paraphrase in the record language. Its original goes into an excerpt ([../excerpts.md](../excerpts.md), whose `find` locates the transcript), unless the fact already cites a file in `sources/` that holds it, and the fact keeps its `(user, YYYY-MM-DD)` and gains the excerpt's key. A fact that cites a session any other way than by an excerpt key gets its excerpt the same way. The paraphrase then takes the belongs test like any other detail: one that belongs stays with its key, and one that does not is deleted under *Condense*, its key staying as the link.
   - **Place every pending line** in the section it belongs to, then delete it. A line's leading date is the day it was recorded: it becomes "as of <date>" on a number a later measurement can replace (and that later measurement replaces it in place), and disappears otherwise, except as the source date of a fact the user stated (`(user, YYYY-MM-DD)`). A date for an event goes into the body only when evidence gives it, and only where an output could use it (the `period`, a launch month).
   - **Settle conflicts the material or the user resolves.** Write the right value in its section with its source, and collapse the conflict to one `Settled:` line in one of the forms `entries/_template.md` gives under `## Conflicts`: the do-not-use form, for a claim with no support, has a quoted claim and a why-clause that name the false claim itself, not the technique or topic it mentions ([../writing.md](../writing.md) › Principles 3). Drop the list of versions; the sources keep them. Evidence settles what is true, including that both sides are (the both-right form); a pick between competing true wordings, or a conflict an approved output merely sidestepped, is the user's ([SKILL.md › Current truth only](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)). Every other conflict stays as a line starting `Open:` (add the marker to an older line that lacks it).
   - **Trim `## Past phrasings`** to at most two wordings per claim per angle (the claim and the angle as `entries/_template.md` defines them). Of near-identical wordings keep one. Rank a wording from an approved output above an imported one, then the newer above the older, and keep the top two. Each keeps one citation; delete anything else there: usage notes, extra citations, and the dates, companies or postings it was used for outside the citation. Names inside the verbatim wording stay.
   - **Keep `links:` to repo roots and main sites**, one each, as its comment in `entries/_template.md` says: a single PR, issue or commit listed there moves into the body as the citation of the fact it supports, or is deleted when no fact cites it, and its repo root is added to `links:` when missing. A root stays even when the body cites it: `tools/deny_coverage.py` reads names from `links:` only.
   - **Make `tags` cover the body:** every main technology, tool, domain and competency the body shows the user using gets a tag, in the form the other entries already use. fit and write select entries from the tags `tools/entries.py list` prints ([fit.md › Select entries](fit.md#select-entries)).
   - **Re-cite facts that cite an output** (a resume, report or answer) with the source in that output's provenance table; when the output has since been deleted, read its table at the cited commit (`git -C <root> show <sha>:<path>`).
   - **Deny the names the front matter records.** Whenever this run creates an entry file or changes an entry's `org`, `title` or `links:` (in this step, a split at step 4 or a correction at step 5), deny the names of each entry file this run created or whose front matter it changed, as *Names appended to `.export-deny`* in [SKILL.md › Commits](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode) says, without waiting for step 5's approval.
3. **Interview the user on the gaps**, in rounds. Each round asks every open question that does not depend on another open answer, numbered, with a recommended answer the user can accept or correct. A recommended answer comes only from the log: a sourced fact, or a reading of one marked `(interpretation, not stated)`, with its source named. For a fact only the user knows (who did which part, who the others were, why a decision went one way), the recommended answer is "unknown": never a plausible guess, because a guess the user accepts becomes a sourced fact. Ask every one of the question types below that the entry has a gap for, not only the first few. Prioritise in this order:
   - attribution (what exactly did *I* do versus others or tools),
   - visibility, when the front matter says `visibility: GAP`: public, private or nda ([SKILL.md › Visibility](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)). Write the answer as its `visibility`, with `# (user, YYYY-MM-DD)` as the line's only comment,
   - open conflicts: which side is right,
   - the reasons behind decisions and the alternatives rejected,
   - people: feedback received, disagreements, who was helped,
   - what was learned and what they would do differently,
   - the questions an interviewer is most likely to ask, with draft answers.
   Record each answer with `(user, YYYY-MM-DD)` in the section it belongs to. An unanswerable question stays a `GAP:` with a note. Record "user does not remember" only for a question the user was asked and answered that way; a question not asked stays a plain `GAP:`. If the user says the experience did not happen or does not count, follow *Dropped experiences* in SKILL.md instead of finishing the entry.
4. **Split an entry still over the threshold.** Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py check <root>` on the draft. Only an `entry over threshold:` line naming this entry matters here; with none, go to step 5. The belongs test alone decides each deletion, so an entry holding only what belongs and still over the threshold covers more than one phase, and the remedy is a split, never a deeper cut. Propose the split and wait for the user's reply:
   - **The unit** is what a reader picks for one posting: a project within a long job, a phase within a long project. Cut where the goal, the role or the scope changed, on a month the evidence gives; when no evidence dates a cut, ask the user for its month in the same message. A cut no one can date is not made: propose another, or keep the entry whole.
   - **For each part,** give its file name, title, period, `## One line` and the sections it takes. A part that would still be over the threshold gets a further cut in the same proposal.

   When the user says no, go to step 5 with the one entry. On their yes to the cut points, or to the ones they corrected, write the parts:
   - **Files.** `<slug>` is the series slug: the entry's own `series:` value when it already has one, else its file name without `<YYYY-MM>-` and `.md`. The earliest part keeps the entry's path, so every reference to it still resolves. Each later part is `entries/<YYYY-MM>-<slug>-<part>.md`: its start month, the series slug, and a short slug naming its phase. Every part, the earliest included, has `series: <slug>` and the title `<series title>: <phase>`, where the series title is the entry's title (the part before `: <phase>` when the entry is already a part).
   - **Each part stands alone:** its own `## One line`, `## Context`, `## My contribution` with all three categories, period, tags and `last_updated`, and the sections its facts fill; a finished part's `## How it works` states the design as that phase left it. A fact two parts need is written in each, in the words that part needs. `org`, `visibility` and `links:` are the same in every part. Each period is `YYYY-MM – YYYY-MM` or `YYYY-MM – ongoing`, with `YYYY-MM-DD` in place of `YYYY-MM` where the evidence gives the day; periods meet at most in the month (or, with days, the day) where one part ends and the next starts, two parts that start in the same month both give days, and only the latest part may be `ongoing`.
   - **Move, not condense.** Every fact, `## Past phrasings` line and `## Conflicts` line goes with its source into the part it is about, placed by the dates and citations the entry already holds (step 1's rule on reopening sources holds here too); a `Settled:` line goes into every part that holds the claim it rules on.
   - **References.** Replace the entry's `README.md` row with one row per part, as step 6 says for a series. Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py refs <entry> <root>`, and point each line in `stories.md` and `profile.md` it lists at the part that now holds what the line names. Each Talking points file in `stories/` that points at the entry (step 6 says when) goes on step 6's list with a **part note**: each such line and the part that now holds what the line names. Its content is the user's to confirm, so it stays as it is. Leave lines in application folders, `sources/` and other files as they are: those are frozen or historical, and the path they name still resolves.

   Run `check` again: done when it prints no `series` finding and no README finding for any part, and no `entry over threshold:` line for any part, unless the user kept that part whole.
5. **Show the user the entry's diff** (`git -C <root> diff -- <entry>`: the updated entry alone hides what was deleted and merged; after a split, `git -C <root> add -N -- <part>…` then `git -C <root> diff -- <entry> <part>… README.md stories.md profile.md`, so the new files and the repointed references show), the new excerpts, and a list of each deletion and merge with the *Condense* rule it follows, a detail moved into an excerpt included. After a split, the list also says which part each moved fact, `## Past phrasings` line and `## Conflicts` line went to, apart from the deletions. Each time this step shows the diff (after a correction or a rejected split too), paste the command's output verbatim into your message, in a fenced `diff` block whose fence is longer than any run of backticks in the diff, then the new excerpts, then the list: running the command shows its output only to you, and a summary in its place hides the hunks. A bulk run pastes each entry's diff at that entry's step 5; the full-diff read that *Bulk data changes* ([SKILL.md › Commits](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode)) asks for comes later, before the bulk yes. Then stop and wait for their reply. When they reject the split itself, `git -C <root> rm -q --cached -- <part>…`, delete those files, put their content back in the entry, restore its README row and the pointers step 4 moved, take the files step 4 put on step 6's list, with their part notes, off it (step 6 lists them again from the restored entry), and show the one entry again. Apply their corrections to everything this step shows and to the part notes, and show the result again if they changed anything. The diff is **approved** when the user says yes to the last version shown, so each correction restarts the wait. A change to an approved entry, at a bulk change's yes included, sends it back through this step. Everything that describes the result, apart from what this step shows the user (after a split, step 4's README rows and pointers), waits for that approval: its size or `check` numbers, a README note, a line in another file's `## Pending`, a report, a follow-up or issue, and the commit (steps 6 and 7 start only after it). Write each from the approved files, and take any size or `check` number from a new run on them: step 4's run on the draft only decides the split.
6. **Update the indexes and list Talking points.** `README.md` row (period, tags, status `consolidated`, with a short note in parentheses if gaps remain). A series has one row per part, kept together, the latest part on top; each row's title, period, type and tags match that part's front matter. Then `stories.md`:
   - **Finish raw and pending Stories.** Run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py story-finish <entry> <root>` on this entry, or on each part after a split. It drops the markers `raw` and `pending` from each item that links the entry at one of its sections, and prints each item for you to repoint or leave:
     - `links no section:` an item marked `raw` or `pending` that links the entry with no section, or with one the entry no longer has, or that links `#pending`. Repoint it, or delete it as below.
     - `raw source:` an item marked `raw` that links a file in `sources/` the entry cites. Repoint it only when the story its label and summary name is this entry's (any part, after a split); leave another entry's as it is. The tool finds a source only by the path the entry writes, so read the index too for a `raw` item that links a source the entry cites by its key, and treat it the same way.

     Each item you repoint now links the section that holds its facts, in whichever part holds them (the section with the user's actions, when they are spread over several), labelled `<that part's title> › <section>`; its markers and summary stay as they are. When the row already holds that link, merge the two at the earlier item's place, keeping both items' markers and the existing item's summary (the repointed one's when only it has one). When no section holds its facts any longer (step 2 deleted them or made them a link), delete the item; if it was the experience's only `core` item, move `core` to another of its items, or, with none left, say that it is no longer core. Then run `story-finish` again: it drops the markers from the items you repointed.
   - **Add this entry** (each part, after a split) under every behavioural question type it can answer, for each section that type's row does not link yet. Then run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py story-coverage <root>`: a `story under too many types:` line for a link this step added or repointed goes to the user with the types and your proposal of which to leave out; wait for their reply and take out the ones they leave out before step 7. Every item this step writes is in the Story index format of the docstring at the top of `tools/entries.py` (*Stories*), with its escapes.

   Then **list the Talking points files** that point at this entry: run `python3 -I <root>/tools/entries.py refs <entry> <root>` on this entry (on each part, after a split); a file in `stories/` points at it when one of the lines `refs` lists for it, read in the file, links the entry outside an HTML comment. A file already on the list from step 4 stays there once, with its part note. Tell the user each file that points at this entry, with its question type, any of its links to `#pending` (they now lead to an empty section), and that the [stories](stories.md) mode reconfirms it; with no `stories/` folder, or no such file, say in one line that no Talking points point at it. The files stay as they are: their content is the user's to confirm.

   Done when the README row (each part's) is updated, the last `story-finish` run on this entry, or on each part after a split, printed `no story index:` or no `links no section:` line, and each `raw source:` line it printed names an item another entry holds, every type this entry answers links it (except those the user left out), and the user has answered any `story under too many types:` line for a link this step wrote, been told each deleted item with its question type and the list of Talking points files that point at it, or that there are none.
7. **Commit and push**, only after step 5, the entry (every part, after a split), the indexes and the new excerpts, each added by name: `consolidate(<slugs>): <summary>`, where `<slugs>` is the slug of every entry file the commit changes (its file name without `<YYYY-MM>-` and `.md`), comma-separated: `links-since` finds an entry's last consolidate by its own slug, a split's later parts and a bulk run's entries included. When one request consolidates more than one entry, it is a bulk change: commit once every entry has been through step 5, on the user's yes, as *Bulk data changes* in [SKILL.md › Commits](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-mode) says.

Done when every section is filled or carries an explicit `GAP:` the user could not answer, every detail belongs or is a link, every quote of the user is replaced by a paraphrase citing its excerpt (a `(user, YYYY-MM-DD)` fact without a key is one step 3 recorded or one whose words no excerpt can hold), every deletion and merge appeared in step 5's list with its *Condense* rule (and, after a split, every moved line with its part), `links:` holds only repo roots and main sites, an entry step 4 found over the threshold is split as the user approved (with no `series` or README finding from `check`) or kept whole on their no, `## Pending` is empty, `## Conflicts` holds only `Open:` and `Settled:` lines, `## Past phrasings` holds at most two wordings per claim per angle, every number has its measurement and source, the attribution section names all three categories (even if one is "none"), and `stories.md` points at this entry (each part, after a split) for every story it holds, step 6's own Done-when holds, and the change is committed and pushed (for a bulk change the user said no to: undone).
