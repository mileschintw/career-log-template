# Speaking standards

Rules for answers the user says aloud to behavioural interview questions: Talking points and the feedback on a mock interview follow them. Written outputs (resumes, form answers, cover letters) follow [writing.md](writing.md) instead; `write` and the review pass check drafts against that file only. Where a preference the user states disagrees with a rule here, the user's preference wins.

Each rule names where it comes from (all read 2026-10-22):
- **TIH**: the Tech Interview Handbook's [behavioral interview guide](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/) and its [guide for senior candidates](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-senior-candidates/);
- **HI**: Hello Interview's behavioral course: [introduction](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/overview/introduction), [choosing responses](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/course/select-choosing-responses-strategically), [telling a good story](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/course/deliver-telling-a-good-story) (partly paywalled; only the open text is used);
- **iio**: interviewing.io's [behavioral interview guide for a large tech employer](https://interviewing.io/guides/amazon-behavioral-interview) and [how Meta evaluates behavioral interviews](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-software-engineering-behavioral-interviews-are-evaluated-meta);
- **employer**: the same employer's own [interview tips](https://aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-interview-best-tips) and [interview mistakes](https://aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-jobs-interview-mistakes);
- **jacobian**: Jacob Kaplan-Moss, [the project-you-led question](https://jacobian.org/2021/feb/9/interview-questions-project-you-led/), written from the interviewer's side;
- **Kao**: Wes Kao, [keep your backstory short](https://newsletter.weskao.com/p/in-job-interviews-keep-your-backstory);
- **practice**: the author's calls and observations while preparing real interviews with this skill;
- **feedback**: advice from recruiters, interviewers or peers.

## Telling a Story

1. **Context is the minimum the listener needs to follow the actions:** 2–3 sentences, about 10–15 seconds, then straight to what the user did. (Kao; HI)
2. **About 80% of the telling is the user's own actions,** each said with "I"; "we" covers only what others did. (iio; employer)
3. **Scope rides along with the actions:** how many people the work needed and which teams were involved, since scope is what separates junior, senior and staff answers. (iio; TIH)
4. **The Story ends on its lesson:** what the user learned, or would do differently next time. (TIH, its extra R for Reflection; HI)
5. **A senior or complex Story leads with its impact:** the result goes in the opening, and the actions explain how it was reached. (TIH)

## Length and follow-ups

6. **Four minutes or less** per answer, the ceiling the employer's own guidance sets; it replaces the common "2–3 minutes", which has no reliable source. (employer)
7. **The first telling stays short; depth comes from follow-ups.** An interviewer may spend 10–15 minutes on one Story, asking the user's role, how problems were spotted and corrected, and what they would do differently. The answers to those live in the entry's `## Likely questions`, ready to give when asked. (employer; iio; jacobian)

## Preparing Stories

8. **Prepare Stories, not scripts:** the user speaks from cue lines and practises saying them aloud, so the wording comes fresh each telling. (TIH; employer)
9. **3–5 core Stories with high impact, high complexity and high personal involvement,** each able to show several signals, plus 5–7 gap fillers (HI) for the question types the core ones miss. (TIH; HI)
10. **One Story can serve several question types from different angles,** but each question in one interview gets its own accomplishment, so the interviewer hears enough different evidence. (TIH; employer)
