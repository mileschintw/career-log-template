# Speaking standards

Rules for what the user says aloud in an interview: answers to behavioural questions, which Talking points, a cue card and the feedback on a mock interview follow, and a prepared talk (*A prepared talk*). Written outputs (resumes, form answers, cover letters) follow [writing.md](writing.md) instead; `write` and the review pass check drafts against that file only. Where a preference the user states disagrees with a rule here, the user's preference wins.

Each rule names where it comes from (all read 2026-10-22):
- **TIH**: the Tech Interview Handbook's [behavioral interview guide](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/) and its [guide for senior candidates](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-senior-candidates/);
- **HI**: Hello Interview's behavioral course: [introduction](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/overview/introduction), [choosing responses](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/course/select-choosing-responses-strategically), [telling a good story](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/course/deliver-telling-a-good-story) (partly paywalled; only the open text is used);
- **iio**: interviewing.io's [behavioral interview guide for a large tech employer](https://interviewing.io/guides/amazon-behavioral-interview) and [how Meta evaluates behavioral interviews](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-software-engineering-behavioral-interviews-are-evaluated-meta);
- **employer**: the same employer's own [interview tips](https://aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-interview-best-tips) and [interview mistakes](https://aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-jobs-interview-mistakes);
- **jacobian**: Jacob Kaplan-Moss, [the project-you-led question](https://jacobian.org/2021/feb/9/interview-questions-project-you-led/), written from the interviewer's side;
- **Kao**: Wes Kao, [keep your backstory short](https://newsletter.weskao.com/p/in-job-interviews-keep-your-backstory);
- **practice**: the author's calls and observations while preparing real interviews with this skill;
- **feedback**: advice from recruiters, interviewers or peers.

## Telling a Story

1. **Context is the minimum the listener needs to follow the actions:** 2–3 sentences, about 10–15 seconds, then straight to what the user did. (Kao; HI)
2. **About 80% of the telling is the user's own actions,** each said with "I"; "we" covers only what others did. (iio; employer)
3. **Scope rides along with the actions:** how many people the work needed and which teams were involved, since scope is what separates junior, senior and staff answers. (iio; TIH)
4. **The Story ends on its lesson:** what the user learned, or would do differently next time. (TIH, its extra R for Reflection; HI)
5. **A senior or complex Story leads with its impact:** the result goes in the opening, and the actions explain how it was reached. (TIH)

## Length and follow-ups

6. **Four minutes or less** per answer, the ceiling the employer's own guidance sets; it replaces the common "2–3 minutes", which has no reliable source. (employer)
7. **The first telling stays short; depth comes from follow-ups.** An interviewer may spend 10–15 minutes on one Story, asking the user's role, how problems were spotted and corrected, and what they would do differently. The answers to those live in the entry's `## Likely questions`, ready to give when asked. (employer; iio; jacobian)

## Preparing Stories

8. **Prepare Stories, not scripts:** the user speaks from cue lines and practises saying them aloud, so the wording comes fresh each telling. (TIH; employer)
9. **3–5 core Stories with high impact, high complexity and high personal involvement,** each able to show several signals, plus 5–7 gap fillers (HI) for the question types the core ones miss. (TIH; HI)
10. **About five deep Stories, each told from different sides, serve a whole loop;** having only one or two is the red flag (feedback). One interviewer hears each accomplishment once, so that interviewer gets enough different evidence; across interviewers a Story may repeat (practice). Practise telling one Story to two different question types, each telling opening from its own question's angle. (TIH; employer; feedback; practice)

## A prepared talk

For a talk the stage asks for ([interview-talk.md](modes/interview-talk.md)), and its rehearsal.

11. **The prompt's rules rule the talk:** what it says about notes, devices, slides and handouts, its suggested structure and its timings per part. A part that runs past the prompt's guide is cut to it. (practice)
12. **Rehearse up a ladder: script, presenter notes, then keywords if the user wants them.** The user rehearses from the full script until they know it, then from presenter notes (each part's points with their key phrases, a few lines word for word, marks for pause, draw and point), and delivers from the notes. A card of five keywords alone is too coarse to deliver from. Rule 8 is for Stories; a talk follows this ladder. (practice)
13. **Aim for the middle of the window.** The target is the window's middle (9 minutes for 8–10); a run that only stays inside the window by watching the clock is too long. Each part's window is set from a spoken, timed run, not from reading the script aloud; a window planned before one is a first guess. (practice)
14. **Check each run:** the total and each part's time against its window; from a recording, when there is one, the pace and the pauses held; any fact said differently from the script; a line the user cannot say comfortably, rewritten until they can. (practice)
15. **Questions after the talk are sized by what the audience does with it.** When the prompt or the research says there are questions, each likely one gets one point in a sentence or two; when the talk is only watched as a presentation, none are prepared. (practice)
16. **Ready when the final text has had one full timed run** that lands within half a minute of the target without the user watching the clock, with every part inside its window, the opening and the last two lines said word for word, no fact said differently from the script and no line the user stumbles on. Any change to the text after that run needs one more full run. (practice)

## The order of a telling

For every telling of a Story, beside rules 1–5.

17. **The employer's answer order comes first.** When the employer states an order for answers (its interview guide's parts, say), the telling follows it, the order the interviewer records in, and is practised until it comes fluently; else the order the user states; else rules 1–5. (feedback)
18. **One task sentence:** after the context and before the actions, one sentence of the user's own role or goal ("my job was to…"), whatever the order. Feedback on a telling without it names this rule. (practice)
